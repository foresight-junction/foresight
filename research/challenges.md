# Junction 2026: All Challenges Summary

Scraped from the Junction platform dashboard (hackjunction.app/participate/45/challenges) on Fri 13 Nov 2026. 19 challenges in total. Each challenge page showed status "Closed" with a countdown at the time of scraping.

## Part 1: Challenges at a glance

1. **Decode the Noise (by Hostinger)**  
   Build an AI-powered tool that detects propaganda, bot networks or bad actors online, and have it running live on Hostinger's infrastructure with a public URL by the end of the hackathon. They care most about strong, honest detection logic (precision, false-positive handling, explainability) rather than a polished front end.
2. **Break the Agent (by AfterQuery)**  
   Build a reinforcement learning environment (game, simulation, software task, real process...) where today's frontier AI agents measurably fail, and prove your team can do better with a reproducible reference run. Your reward function must hold up against judges actively trying to reward-hack it.
3. **Earned Trust in AI Code (by Tieto)**  
   Design an AI code review concept for pull requests written by AI agents, then show it working on one sample agent-written change. Tieto cares most about a clear concept with explicit trade-offs where a named human stays accountable, backed by evidence they can check.
4. **Make Unicorns Real (by Cala)**  
   Build a product for founders and/or investors on top of Cala's structured, sourced public data (companies, people, funding rounds, investors). Cala wants one real need solved well and end to end, with Cala at the core, ideally usable the day after the hackathon (Slush starts right after).
5. **Spread (by Bahn Express)**  
   Build a system that runs a simulated car transport company for a week in Finland: quote dealers, win cars, post drives as gigs to freelance drivers and set the fee. Score is gross margin (dealer payments minus driver pay) with at least 90% of cars arriving on time; the top 10 then present to a jury.
6. **Trusted Trade Agents (by Valio Aimo)**  
   Prototype the interface and operating model that lets an external AI purchasing agent find products, compare offers and place an order with Valio Aimo, with clear rules on what the agent may decide alone versus what needs human approval. They care most about a working end-to-end flow (request, lookup, order/approval) on mocked systems, built on open standards.
7. **The Internet Butler / Your Personal Assistant (by Exa)**  
   Build a personal agent that takes tedious, research-heavy life tasks off people's hands, using Exa web search to find information a model could not know from training. It should get the task as close to done as possible and leave final approval to the user. Judges care about real use of Exa, usefulness to real people, clear sources, originality and a demo that works on live queries.
8. **The Green European Renaissance (by Arduino)**  
   Build a physical Edge AI prototype on the Arduino UNO Q (with Modulino sensors) that solves a local problem and helps the environment and people's lives. Judges weigh technical use of the UNO Q, sensors and on-device AI, plus environmental and social impact, most heavily. You must also publish the project on the Arduino Project Hub to be prize-eligible.
9. **AI Glasses for Everyone, Everyday (by Meta)**  
   Build the best everyday use case for Meta AI glasses, showing how a first-person view enables new or better hands-free interactions, with a functional prototype and a deck. Judges score equally on creativity, technical execution, real-world value and user experience (input, accessibility, trust).
10. **Stop Simulating, Start Moving: See It. Detect It. Act on It. The Intel Physical AI Challenge (by Ignite Next x Intel)**  
   Using a real SO-101 robot arm, teach it by teleoperation to pick Duplo bricks, show them to an inspection camera, and sort them into "good" or "defective" bowls fully autonomously. You train a VLA policy and an Anomalib defect model, then run both optimized with OpenVINO on an Intel Core Ultra Series 3 laptop. Judges care most about task success and how well you integrate Intel technology.
11. **The Robot Teleoperation Challenge (by Rokoko)**  
   Build an impressive application of robot teleoperation, using Rokoko motion capture suits and gloves to control robotic hands, arms or humanoid robots, either physically or in simulation. The topic is open; they want something useful, unexpected or surprising, and they score technical execution highest.
12. **Aaltonomous Humanoid (by Aalto University)**  
   Write the "brain" that makes a Unitree G1 humanoid sort small colored objects into boxes on its own, within 2 minutes, with no human control. You build and test in an Isaac Sim simulation, submit a Docker image, and the best finalists run on the real robot on Sunday. Real-robot performance counts most (40%).
13. **Equal Care Across Finland (by AstraZeneca)**  
   Build an AI-powered system that finds where cancer care in Finland is unequal, shows where in the care pathway the gap appears, and above all recommends concrete actions. It must run as a live demo, use public data with clear sources, and cover at least one Goal 3 indicator of the national cancer strategy.
14. **Beyond the Clinic (by Aalto University)**  
   Build a product that helps people stay well at home after TMS treatment for depression, using anything from phones, music, games, light or AI. Ideally it works as a closed loop: sense how the person is doing, respond with something helpful, and learn what works for that person. No special hardware is needed.
15. **Make Blueprints Talk (by Stoa)**  
   Build a program that answers natural language questions about real construction drawings and building models. Answers must be correct and show where in the drawing they came from so a human can check them in seconds. Stoa wants approaches that frontier models alone will not make obsolete next year.
16. **The Web Is Your Real-Time Database (by Context.dev)**  
   Build a product that reacts to the live web and turns live web data into decisions, actions or experiences that were not possible before. Each team gets free Context.dev API credits to do it. They reward real business or consumer need, creative combination of sources, and products that react to change over time rather than one-off snapshots.
17. **Rethinking Recruitment (by City of Espoo)**  
   Imagine and prototype new ways to help companies and talent find relevant, meaningful connections at recruitment and matchmaking events run by the City of Espoo, even though AI-generated job materials and AI-assisted screening are now common. The solution can cover any stage before, during or after an event. They care most about impact and relevance, and about responsible, privacy-safe design with no automated decisions about individuals.
18. **Wikipedia Through Time. (by MariaDB)**  
   Build a tool for Wikipedia that doesn't exist yet (or is badly underused) with MariaDB at the core, in one of two tracks: RAG with MariaDB vector search, or system-versioned tables for time-travel over article history. They want the most useful and original tool, with effective use of MariaDB, scalability across datasets, and a good demo.
19. **Personal Economic Trainer (by S-Pankki)**  
   Build an AI experience, ideally mobile, that lets people chat with their own financial data using a large synthetic S-Bank-style transaction dataset, acting as a trusted personal financial coach. They care about financial literacy (with a teaching angle), original AI use, clear mobile UX, feasibility and fit with the S-mobiili app.

### Break the Agent (by AfterQuery)
**Link:** https://hackjunction.app/participate/45/challenges/328
**Summary:** Build a reinforcement learning environment (game, simulation, software task, real process...) where today's frontier AI agents measurably fail, and prove your team can do better with a reproducible reference run. Your reward function must hold up against judges actively trying to reward-hack it.

**The problem / context:** Frontier AI can do impressive things but still struggles in many novel everyday or niche technical situations. AfterQuery builds training and testing environments for agents and wants to see your take on choosing a problem, action/observation space and reward.

**What to build (requirements & must-haves):**
- An environment an agent can interact with: clear goal, actions, checkable reward
- Explain why you chose the setting and what an agent could learn there
- Run a frontier agent in it and compare against a stronger reference run your team can demonstrate and reproduce
- Show where and why the agent falls short; difficulty must come from decisions, with the needed information and tools available
- Reward must resist shortcuts (reward hacking); doing nothing or stopping early should earn little
- Optional stretch goal: train a smaller model on experience from your environment and show whether it improves

**Suggested directions / focus areas:** Setting is open: a game, scientific simulation, software task, real-world process or something new.

**Data, APIs, tools & credits provided:** AfterQuery provides the agent harness and model credits.

**Submission / deliverables:**
- 10-minute demo per team
- README from which judges can run the evaluation with one command and reproduce reported results

**Judging criteria:**
- The gap (30 p): frontier agent consistently scores below your reference run, reproducible across repeated runs
- Meaningful difficulty (30 p): agent mistakes explainable and tied to task decisions; no unfairly withheld info, trick wording, impossible timing or broken tools
- Reward integrity (25 p): reward survives judges' exploit attempts; completing the task well is the best way to score
- Craft (15 p): sensible rules/behaviour; one-command reproducible evaluation
- How judging runs: after the demo, judges rerun with the standard harness: your reference run, three agent runs, a do-nothing baseline and a stop-early baseline, plus 15 minutes hunting for reward exploits
- Tiebreaker: a smaller model shown improving after training on your environment

**Prizes:**
- 1st: 3,000 € + work trial at AfterQuery (skip straight to final interview stage) + merch
- 2nd: 1,500 € + merch
- 3rd: 500 €
- Finalists get AfterQuery gear

**Mentors / extras:** Winning team members can go straight to the final stage of AfterQuery's interview process (work trial with the environments team).

### Earned Trust in AI Code (by Tieto)
**Link:** https://hackjunction.app/participate/45/challenges/351
**Summary:** Design an AI code review concept for pull requests written by AI agents, then show it working on one sample agent-written change. Tieto cares most about a clear concept with explicit trade-offs where a named human stays accountable, backed by evidence they can check.

**The problem / context:** Agent tokens passed human tokens in February 2026 and reached 7.3 trillion by August (OpenRouter data, a16z chart). Reviewing every change by hand does not scale, yet engineers need a sound basis to trust AI-reviewed code. The question: how does AI code review keep pace with AI-written code while humans stay accountable for every change? The idea: a PR opens with its own explanation (what it does, why, what else it touches, how sure the AI is about each part), so the reviewer checks a story instead of hunting for problems.
**What to build (requirements & must-haves):**
- Define the concept: what the team recommends the "PR review" factory should follow.
- State key principles, including how it balances risk, hallucination, transparency, confidence and quality.
- Illustrate it as a reference on one sample agent-written change with its requirement ID.
- Show what the AI found, what it refused to decide, and why.
- Show how the evidence lets a named human accept the change without a full read.
- Name the failure modes seen, and keep a short log of decisions.
- Cover concept, principles, reference implementation and technology considerations across frontend, backend and security.
- Challenge the traditional model: is line-by-line review still right?
- The result is a reference implementation that illustrates the concept, not a product.
- Use synthetic or cleared sample data only.
- Out of scope: a finished product, model training, vendor selection.
**Suggested directions / focus areas:**
- Generalist reviewer: one reviewer covering frontend, backend, security and performance (how to train or prompt for that breadth is part of the work).
- Classifier: flag code that genuinely needs human review and separate it from code that meets the standard.
- Options, not requirements; every option keeps a named human accountable.
- Principles from the Insight section: a named engineer owns and signs each change; AI review is advice with evidence, never the approval; evidence traceable to a requirement; depth of human review follows risk; humans spot-check the AI reviewer and track misses; trust is earned per area from track record and withdrawn after escapes.
- What the owner signs: what was checked and by whom/what; what was not checked and why; the residual risk accepted. Not accepted: "The AI said it was fine", an unowned merge, confidence without evidence, or an unexplained risk score.
- Vendor recommendations cited: route human attention by risk, keep reviews fast and low-noise; review with full repo context and verify findings before posting; measure outcomes (reverts, incidents), not review activity.
- Questions judges will ask: What would make you not trust your own output? Who owns the decision? How would this scale?
**Data, APIs, tools & credits provided:**
- Background on what good code means at Tieto: standards, security and quality.
- A requirements set: intent, functional and non-functional.
- Sample agent-written changes, each with its requirement ID.
- Model access: models from other Junction partners via partner credits on the Junction Platform.
- Code repo (make your own copy first): https://github.com/te-industry-tech-office/junction26-messagepack-csharp
